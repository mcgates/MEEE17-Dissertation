This dissertation has explored the impact of renewable energy integration on DC grid stability and efficiency. Through a comprehensive analysis of residential energy management and control systems, this research has demonstrated that such systems can significantly enhance the utilization of renewable resources while maintaining grid stability.\par

\section{Key findings}
\subsection{Efficiency in Energy Utilisation}
The EMS enhances system efficiency by adapting to real-time consumption patterns, preventing overloading and excessive battery discharge. This optimization not only improves energy usage but also extends battery life. The system of systems architecture supports this by ensuring each unit operates independently, prioritising local resources while sharing any excess energy and storage when not in use.
\subsection{Power Sharing}
The power sharing feature allows surplus energy from fully charged local battery storage to be redirected to the grid. This helps in rapid recharging and supplying connected loads, enhancing overall energy distribution efficiency. Power sharing is also utilised in systems with PV panels during daylight when they are generating power and there's minimal load connected to the system.\par
For units with battery storage and PV panels, power sharing is enabled when sufficient power is available to charge the battery while also supplying local loads. Simulation and lab results have demonstrated that the power sharing feature is activated for varying durations once the conditions are met.
\subsection{Grid Dependence and Battery Capacity}

The laboratory results for Unit 2 reveal that grid reliance for battery charging is crucial due to limited battery capacity and the absence of PV modules. This research has utilised PV panels for energy generation and lithium batteries for storage, highlighting the significant reliance on battery capacity since PV panels do not produce power during nighttime. To stabilise the grid, additional energy storage solutions are essential. A diverse range of energy sources and storage technologies enhances grid stability and reliability.\par
Prioritising grid usage over local storage allows for extended operation of local loads; however, the rapid decrease in the state of charge necessitates frequent switching to grid power. From the grid’s perspective, connected systems function as both energy sources and loads, managing themselves according to predefined rules to prevent total grid collapse. Supply and demand analyses underscore the importance of self-regulation in addressing imbalances.\par
\subsection{Recommendations}
This research contributes valuable empirical evidence to the field, supporting the efficiency and reliability of Renewable Energy Management and Control Systems in renewable energy systems. Future work could explore the implementation of predictive artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms to improve REMCS and anticipate supply and demand fluctuations more effectively. Incorporating additional renewable energy sources and advanced storage technologies into the grid could further stabilise operations and reduce dependence on any single energy source.\par
The introduction of USB Type-C and the Power Delivery (PD) standard has enabled the development of new DC home appliances, eliminating the need for inverters and transformers. DC microgrids offer an efficient solution for integrating these appliances with distributed energy sources and storage systems.
The findings highlight the potential of DC microgrids for home electrification. A "system of systems" approach provides a comprehensive framework for understanding and optimising the interaction between these interconnected systems, paving the way for a sustainable energy future.
